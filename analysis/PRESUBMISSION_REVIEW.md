# Adversarial pre-submission review

## Review setup

- **Input scope:** Complete English Full-paper draft, Supplementary
  Information, five main figures, frozen designs and amendments, compact
  result files, related-work audit, citation verification, and release tests.
- **Assessment boundary:** Stringent reviewer-style assessment of the current
  scientific case, using Nature-style originality, importance, technical
  soundness, interdisciplinary reach, and readability axes. The intended
  journal remains *Digital Discovery*; this is not an editorial decision.
- **Shared manuscript claim summary:** Target-only models are weakest in few-shot
  and OOD regions where observations and mechanistic guidance are sparse.
  Qualified neighboring experimental domains can contribute useful and
  complementary priors to those regions, but heterogeneous aggregation alone
  does not establish universal laws or transportable coefficients. Reported
  experiments are therefore treated as a partial scientific memory—measurements
  linked to identity, conditions, provenance, and reuse constraints—not as
  interchangeable labels. The manuscript operationalizes this
  experimental-first premise through a selective neighborhood-borrowing
  strategy that qualifies sources, matches borrowing to the prediction or OOD
  endpoint, preserves independent source proposals, combines complementary
  shortlists, and rejects harmful sources through shuffled and wrong-domain
  controls. The associated artifact-gated map records positive, null, harmful,
  and unresolved directed edges; it is not a universal predictor of untested
  edges or a claim that experimental labels automatically encode chemistry.
- **Visible evidence base:** 20 numerically analysed experimental resources,
  including a source-pinned integration of 96,184 measurements; 42-edge
  internal map; independent BIRDSHOT and Matbench
  boundaries; frozen KIT within-campaign gain; frozen paper-disjoint CALiSol
  null boundary; thermoelectric and ISODB compensation tests; downstream fixed-
  ranking OOD signal and frozen sequential-discovery null; verified Caltech
  external adaptive-policy null, negative-transfer safety gates, prespecified
  real-neighbor shortlist signal, and a diagnostic two-neighbor portfolio that
  expands external top-region coverage from 2/8 and 3/8 to 5/8; outcome-informed
  CCA family-first allocation recovers 4/4 distinct top external components and
  both 2/2 hard-OOD components while exposing the entity-repeat trade-off;
  complete outcome-unseen Starrydata reverse-transport and four-plate TRI OER
  programmes with matched controls, learner/representation robustness,
  exploration policies, six hypothesis cards, and two-target random effects;
  and an independently reconstructed leave-one-program CCA gate benchmark over
  97 edges, 20 tasks, and 13 programme clusters.
- **Missing materials affecting confidence:** Persistent release DOI, clean
  Linux reproduction record, author/affiliation/CRediT/funding details,
  author-confirmed conflicts statement, RSC typesetting, and table-of-contents
  entry.
- **Missing scientific evidence affecting confidence:** None for the bounded
  retrospective map claim under the authoritative
  `core_story_experiment_registry.json`; every paper-submission-required item is
  complete or complete-boundary. No independent external target passes the full
  positive prediction gate, so the paper cannot claim a replicated general
  transfer effect. The cross-program gate has positive point utility and low
  clear-harm admission but does not beat adjacency-only or the frozen primary
  comparators; it supports a method-development boundary, not a validated
  universal gate. A genuinely temporal or laboratory test is additionally
  required before claiming prospective acceleration, new science, or
  experiments saved.

  The completed formal programme contains both the 7,403-entity Starrydata reverse-
  transport target and the clean 8,447-entity, four-set TRI OER target. The
  initially nominated acid-OER target failed an outcome-free plate-size gate
  after immutable accidental-access exclusions and is sensitivity-only; it
  cannot be counted as an independent confirmation.

## Post-Balam evidence update

The hash-verified OBELiX campaign completed as Job 70624 on Balam and passed the
design, input, environment, seed-coverage, checksum, and completion-sentinel
audits. The named official-test thermoelectric-prior policy saved only 0.25
acquisitions [−1.30,1.82], p=0.3889, and failed every frozen improvement and
rescue gate. The Random-Forest sensitivity was also null. The hard-OOD
ExtraTrees effect was +2.21 [0.97,3.49] experiments, but failed the practical,
relative, consistency, and Random-Forest-sensitivity gates and remains
exploratory. Uniform random acquisition required 15.50 acquisitions, compared
with 24.34 for target-only UCB and 24.09 for thermoelectric-prior UCB.

This result establishes an endpoint boundary rather than negating knowledge
transfer. Under a sequential endpoint prespecified after the fixed-ranking
analysis, a directional OOD signal disappears after acquisition and refitting.
The random-control result reveals that acquisition-policy design, not merely
source-prior choice, can erase useful source information.

The isolated post-result policy benchmark now resolves part of that failure
anatomy: target-mean greedy loses to random, while composition novelty beats
random in both pools. Source/target/novelty fusion improves official-pool
cumulative recall but does not beat novelty for first hit and loses early in
hard OOD. The thermoelectric static rank is only two acquisitions ahead of the
catalysis control, below the source-specificity gate. These results are useful
for selecting an external benchmark but are outcome-selected on OBELiX and do
not by themselves add a confirmatory manuscript claim.

The independent Caltech ionic-conductor benchmark has now completed formal
Balam Job 70740 and same-environment verification Job 70767. None of the
frozen OBELiX, ESTM, or multisource residual increments passes the adaptive
source-policy gates. Composition novelty is beneficial only in hard OOD and
still misses the absolute-recall gate; target-mean steering is harmful there.
All wrong-source harm guards pass, and the admission rule admits designated
neighbors more frequently and at higher weight than wrong controls. This is a
selective safety result, not demonstrated source credibility: OBELiX source OOF
R² is 0.065, whereas the OCx wrong-domain control is highest at 0.543.

The prespecified real-neighbor static rankings exceed every random, shuffled,
mechanical, and catalysis reference in both Caltech scopes after exact formula
and DOI exclusions. They recover 2/8 and 3/8 external top-5% entities and both
recover all 3/3 hard-OOD entities. A post-result OBELiX/ESTM portfolio expands
external coverage to 5/8, demonstrating that the two neighbors make
complementary proposals on the observed target. Because static-source
attribution was not in the frozen primary contrast family and the portfolio was
constructed after outcome inspection, these results establish retrospective
external feasibility and portfolio complementarity, not independent
prospective acceleration. The external experiment does not localize the
adaptive bottleneck uniquely: weak source skill, weak target-surrogate quality,
policy conversion, or their interaction can explain the adaptive null.

The subsequent CCA method-development audit addresses a pseudoreplication risk
in the top-entity endpoint. The eight external top entities occupy four
connected formula/DOI/ICSD components, while all three hard-OOD top entities
belong to one component. Family-first consensus increases distinct-component
AUC20 from 47 to 60 externally and from 36 to 39 in hard OOD, recovering 4/4
and 2/2 top components. Wrong-source AUC20 is 6/18, and 5,000 shuffled-rank
pairs give conditional p=0.0020/0.0030. Candidate-outcome permutation leaves
the order unchanged. This materially strengthens the OOD-exploration story,
but it remains outcome-informed, conditional on one target, and explicitly
reduces entity recall. Its independent Starrydata and TRI tests are now complete
and do not pass the full policy gate.

The final outcome-unseen programme completed as Balam Job 70888 and passed both
portable verifiers. Starrydata produced +0.88% [0.02,1.77%] relative RMSE in the
primary far-OOD cell, but Holm p=0.071, R²=-0.485, and the matched-control and
same-domain comparisons failed; CCA AUC20=41 versus 71 for ESTM and all three
hypothesis cards failed. TRI produced -0.079% [-0.313,0.155%] across four plates,
negative absolute R² on every plate, a -1.25% contrast with the best control,
and no confirmed policy or hypothesis card. The two-target mean was +0.304%
[-0.617,1.225%], I²=76.7%. This closes the scientific execution gate but not by
supplying a second positive: it provides direct evidence that the map must
abstain or reject most attractive neighboring proposals.

## Reviewer 1

- **Overall assessment:** The technical architecture is substantially stronger
  than a conventional transfer-learning demonstration because the manuscript
  separates coefficient transport, prediction borrowing, OOD proposal
  generation, absolute utility, and sample efficiency. KIT supplies material
  predictive transfer, while Caltech shows external OOD signal and
  complementary source proposals. The integrated strategy is therefore
  supported at component level, although its label-saving magnitude and
  prospective policy performance remain less certain than the point results.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why:** Materials-informatics
  researchers, experimental database builders, and scientists using small
  heterogeneous datasets would value a reproducible rule for refusing
  attractive but non-transportable priors.
- **Major strengths:** Entity- and provenance-group exclusions are explicit;
  target-training source features are cross-fitted; same-series fitted
  quantities are forbidden; null and harmful edges are retained; CALiSol is
  not replaced after failure; and the p=0.004 CALiSol mapping result is not
  allowed to override failed practical and absolute-utility gates.
- **Major concerns:** KIT is not independent external replication. Its n=30
  baseline already has R²=0.739, so “rescue” is an operational label rather
  than a transition from unusable to usable. The 37.35% target-label-saving
  point estimate has a post-outcome diagnostic interval of 21.84–49.91%, and
  80.52% of replicates meet the 30% point threshold. The 5% and 30%
  thresholds are operational choices, not physically derived constants. The
  designs were frozen internally but not externally preregistered.
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is
  established:** Do not claim an uncertainty-qualified 30% saving lower bound;
  retain the diagnostic in the main text and figure; keep “rescue” explicitly
  tied to the frozen rule; archive the exact reproducible release; and show a
  clean-environment run before submission. These manuscript changes are now
  present, but the archival and clean-environment steps remain open.
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria:** Originality is credible at the
  workflow and evidence-hierarchy level. Scientific importance is practical
  rather than mechanistic. Interdisciplinary value lies in falsification of
  data-derived priors. Technical soundness is strong for the scoped claims but
  does not support field-level rescue. Readability is good after separating
  point rules from uncertainty-qualified conclusions.
- **Recommendation posture:** Scientifically supportable after the remaining
  reproducibility and submission-package gaps are closed. The cross-domain
  claim should be stated positively as qualified prediction and OOD-proposal
  transfer, but not expanded to field-wide rescue or prospective acceleration.

## Reviewer 2

- **Overall assessment:** Transfer learning, task maps, materials databases,
  and compensation-artifact critiques are not individually new. The defensible
  originality is their integration into one falsifiable, provenance-aware
  borrowing audit in which failures remain first-class results.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why:** Readers working on
  materials acceleration, experimental-data reuse, benchmark design, and
  reliable AI for science would care because the paper shows how to decide
  whether an apparently adjacent dataset should influence a scarce-label
  model.
- **Major strengths:** The Borg coefficient-transport failure provides a clear
  counterexample to in-domain R² rhetoric. BIRDSHOT, Matbench, KIT, and CALiSol
  define distinct evidence layers instead of being pooled as exchangeable
  replications. The ISODB analysis demonstrates that the artifact gate can
  retain a strong but conditional pattern rather than mechanically dismissing
  every pooled relation.
- **Major concerns:** The integrated shortlist portfolio has now been frozen on
  two outcome-unseen targets and neither passes its full gate. BIRDSHOT lacks
  absolute utility, Matbench is null, CALiSol does not preserve the KIT gain,
  and the verified Caltech adaptive residual policies are null. These boundaries
  prevent a claim of prospective field rescue. They do not erase the positive
  component-level case: KIT establishes predictive gain and Caltech establishes
  external source-ranked proposals and observed-target complementarity. The
  20-resource cohort and SQLite layer support the method but are not themselves
  a novelty claim; source-specific reuse terms remain controlling.
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is
  established:** The title, abstract, and conclusion should sell the operational
  map and KIT positive existence result while explicitly separating them from a
  universal transfer law or independent field-level rescue. Prior work must
  remain explicit enough to avoid priority drift. The
  generated database must not be redistributed as an unrestricted artifact
  while ESTM reuse status and the non-commercial source remain unresolved.
  Caltech static rankings must remain explicitly retrospective and outside the
  primary inferential family, and the
  outcome-selected portfolio must be tested on a new target before it can
  become an external source-to-policy edge.
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria:** Originality is strongest in
  composition of the method, not its components. Importance is high for
  trustworthy materials ML but not a new materials law. Interdisciplinary
  interest is plausible because the claim hierarchy applies beyond materials.
  Technical evidence supports selective local usefulness, not a general
  physical neighborhood metric. The current title and abstract are readable
  and appropriately scoped.
- **Recommendation posture:** Promising methods-led Full paper if the authors
  lead with the selective borrowing strategy and its positive prediction/OOD
  evidence, while using failed transport as the causal argument for why the
  gates and endpoint-matched portfolio are necessary. Weak only if retrospective
  feasibility is relabelled as prospective field rescue.

## Reviewer 3

- **Overall assessment:** The manuscript has a comprehensible general message:
  aggregation is not inference, and a useful prior must survive a sequence of
  explicit gates. The main risk for nonspecialists is terminology that makes an
  operational status sound like a physical breakthrough.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why:** Experimental chemists,
  materials scientists, data curators, and ML practitioners can all use the
  distinction between pooled regularity, coefficient transport, knowledge
  borrowing, and downstream discovery acceleration.
- **Major strengths:** The two figures form a coherent evidence chain;
  panel a falsifies coefficient transport, panels b–c show local gain plus its
  uncertainty and transport boundary, and panel d shows why artifact
  gatekeeping is not a universal null generator. Figure 2 separates fixed OOD
  screening from sequential discovery and exposes the random-control advantage
  rather than hiding it. The prose repeatedly states what each result does not
  prove.
- **Major concerns:** “Rescue” remains rhetorically stronger than the observed
  R²=0.739→0.811 improvement, especially because the target-only model is
  already useful. The extensive gate vocabulary may overwhelm readers unless
  Table 1 and Supplementary Table S3 remain tightly aligned. Data availability
  is still incomplete without a persistent release DOI, despite public source
  URLs and hashes.
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is
  established:** Use “materially improves” in the result heading and reserve
  “local task rescue” for the defined status; keep the 21.84–49.91% diagnostic
  beside the 37.35% point; supply an archived release and a concise graphical
  table-of-contents entry; and ensure the RSC Data availability statement names
  the exact version and DOI. The first two changes are now implemented.
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria:** Originality is clear enough for
  a methods contribution. Scientific importance is credible if framed around
  reliable reuse rather than a new law. The general logic is interdisciplinary.
  Technical limitations are visible instead of hidden. Readability is strong,
  although the operational terminology requires discipline.
- **Recommendation posture:** Readable and potentially useful to a broad
  digital-discovery audience once the persistent package and author-facing
  submission materials are complete.

## Cross-review synthesis

- **Consensus strengths:** The manuscript's strongest advance is an executable
  selective borrowing strategy backed by complementary evidence layers. KIT
  demonstrates material few-shot prediction gain; Caltech preserves external
  OOD signal after formula and DOI exclusions; two neighboring sources recover
  different high-value entities; and wrong-source gates suppress irrelevant
  inputs. Leakage control, provenance-group splitting, negative controls, and
  artifact diagnostics make those positive results unusually auditable. The
  sequential failures then identify why source signal must be separated from
  target-surrogate and acquisition-policy quality.
- **Consensus technical risks:** KIT is within-campaign and has a strong
  target-only baseline; the exact label-saving magnitude is uncertain; no
  independently replicated cross-domain rescue exists; thresholds are
  operational; persistent code/data deposition and clean-environment
  reproduction remain incomplete. The Caltech static-ranking evidence lacks a
  frozen attribution family and independent-target uncertainty; its portfolio
  was outcome-selected.
- **Where emphasis differs across reviewers:** Reviewer 1 places greatest
  weight on inference and sample-equivalence uncertainty. Reviewer 2 places
  greatest weight on novelty and the absence of a positive external
  cross-domain edge. Reviewer 3 places greatest weight on the rhetorical gap
  between “rescue” and “improvement,” plus reader-facing data availability.
- **Broad-interest / significance readout:** The broad value is both scientific
  and methodological: qualified neighboring domains can contribute useful,
  nonredundant proposals even when no universal law or single pooled model
  exists. The strategy specifies how to turn those proposals into auditable
  prediction gains, OOD shortlists, and predeclared scientific hypotheses.
- **Most important issues to resolve before a strong case is established:**
  Archive the exact release with a DOI; complete clean Linux reproduction;
  retain the scoped wording and sample-saving diagnostic; and do not claim
  field-level, cross-domain, or independently replicated rescue.

## Risk / unsupported claims

- **Unsupported:** Aggregated data reveal a universal materials law.
- **Unsupported:** The present map rescues an entire data-poor field.
- **Unsupported:** The KIT result is independent cross-campaign replication.
- **Unsupported:** At least 30% target labels are saved with 95% confidence.
- **Unsupported:** Temperature or the 0–3 neighborhood score is a universal
  transfer-distance law.
- **Unsupported:** Source-feature importance identifies microscopic mechanism.
- **Unsupported:** Prediction improvement accelerates active discovery.
- **Unsupported:** The directional OBELiX fixed-ranking signal establishes
  sequential OOD-discovery improvement or rescue.
- **Unsupported:** The hard-OOD ExtraTrees result passes the frozen sequential
  gates, or uniform random acquisition is generally optimal.
- **Unsupported:** Caltech validates adaptive neighbor-driven discovery
  acceleration, or its static rankings/portfolio constitute a confirmed
  source-to-policy edge.
- **Unsupported:** Every analysed source or the generated SQLite database is
  unrestricted for redistribution.

retrospective experiment cycle.** The core-story registry now passes: every
submission-required experiment is complete or complete-boundary. KIT provides a
material predictive-transfer existence result; Caltech provides external OOD

leave-one-program synthesis adds a concrete method result: physical adjacency

sufficient benefit selector, motivating the frozen candidate-local CCA-v2
protocol. For
*Digital Discovery*, this is a defensible methods-led paper if the central claim
is the operational, artifact-gated map of sparse and endpoint-specific borrowing,
not a general transfer effect. Before submission, archive the exact release with
a DOI, run the clean Linux reproduction, complete author metadata and RSC
formatting, and keep the OBELiX random-control failure plus KIT/CALiSol, Caltech,
Starrydata, and TRI boundaries in the main evidence chain. CS13 is not required
for the bounded retrospective claim, but it is mandatory before using
“discovery acceleration”, “new science discovered”, or “laboratory experiments
saved”.
