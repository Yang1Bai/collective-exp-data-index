# ISODB isosteric compensation artifact protocol

Status: designated after metadata-only feasibility audit and before the first
enthalpy--intercept outcome run; not externally preregistered.

## Purpose

Test whether a strong pooled adsorption van't Hoff compensation pattern can be
generated by parameter covariance over narrow temperature windows.  This is an
artifact-control case study, not evidence for a universal adsorption law.

## System and isotherm eligibility

A system is keyed by DOI, adsorbent hash/name, adsorbate InChIKey/name,
adsorption unit, and pressure unit.  Include only:

1. pure-component isotherms with exactly one adsorbate;
2. pressure reported in bar;
3. at least three distinct positive temperatures spanning at least 20 K;
4. at least five finite, positive pressure--uptake points per isotherm;
5. a positive pressure--uptake Spearman correlation of at least 0.90;
6. a non-empty common positive uptake interval across all retained
   temperatures.

No conversion between adsorption units is needed because each fit is performed
within one system and one reported unit.  Pressure is standardized as
`ln(p / 1 bar)`.

## One fit per system

The primary uptake is the geometric midpoint of the common uptake interval.
At each temperature, interpolate `ln(p / bar)` against `ln(uptake)`.  Duplicate
isotherms at one temperature are reduced to the median interpolated pressure.
Fit

`ln(p / bar) = intercept - Q_st / (R T)`.

The primary pool requires fit R-squared at least 0.90 and
`0 < Q_st < 200 kJ mol-1`.  This yields one enthalpy/intercept pair per system,
preventing uptake-level pseudoreplication.

## Compensation and artifact decisions

Regress the dimensionless intercept on `Q_st` with HC3 uncertainty.  For a
positive slope `b` in `(kJ mol-1)^-1`, calculate

`T_iso = 1000 / (R b)`.

The apparent law is artifact-consistent if its isokinetic temperature lies
inside the pooled experimental temperature range and within 10% of the median
system harmonic-mean temperature.  DOI-cluster bootstrap intervals are the
primary uncertainty for pooled slope, R-squared, and `T_iso`.

## Frozen sensitivities

- common log-uptake fractions 0.25, 0.50, and 0.75;
- van't Hoff R-squared cutoffs 0.80, 0.90, and 0.95;
- temperature-span cutoffs 20, 40, and 60 K;
- family-level analyses only for at least eight independent systems and with
  Holm correction.

## Krug null simulation

For the primary retained systems, independently permute fitted intercepts
across fitted `Q_st` values, generate synthetic `ln(p)` on each observed
temperature grid, add Gaussian residual noise using that system's fitted
residual scale, and refit both parameters.  Repeat 1,000 times.  The null keeps
the real temperature design and noise while making the true enthalpy and
intercept independent.  Compare the observed pooled R-squared and `T_iso` with
this artifact null; it is not a mechanistic adsorption simulation.
