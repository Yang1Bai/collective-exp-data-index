## A. Falsifiable Scientific Hypotheses

The following ten hypotheses explain when and why neighboring-domain knowledge should improve OOD scientific decisions. Each is grounded in independently verified mechanisms from the transfer learning, OOD materials ML, and safe-transfer literatures.

**Overarching framework.** The central finding across OOD materials benchmarks is that most purported OOD tasks are actually interpolative—models fail only when test data are representationally outside training support, not merely statistically different ([1.1], [1.2]). This implies that neighboring-domain borrowing should help precisely in those representationally sparse target regions where single-source learning lacks support, and should be harmless or harmful elsewhere. Negative transfer is well-documented as a pervasive risk: the comprehensive survey by Zhang et al. (610 citations) establishes that transfer fails when domains are insufficiently related, distributions differ drastically, or models are improperly applied across domains ([2.1], [2.2]). The robust multi-fidelity Bayesian optimization literature further demonstrates that unreliable auxiliary information sources can degrade optimization below single-fidelity baselines ([3.1], [3.2]). These findings motivate explicit credibility gates, abstention, and condition-specific rather than universal borrowing.

## B. Methods Comparison

The current project uses static rank fusion and RF feature injection. Both are limited: rank fusion ignores confidence, and RF feature injection risks importing nuisance correlations without leakage controls. The literature provides nine alternatives that could outperform these baselines while respecting the validity rules.

The most promising method is **conformal risk-controlled borrowing gates**, because conformal prediction provides finite-sample, distribution-free guarantees and naturally supports abstention under domain shift—exactly the behavior the thesis demands ([10.1], [10.2]). The **rMFBO-style robust approach** is the strongest for sequential acquisition settings, with theoretical guarantees that performance cannot fall below single-fidelity baselines ([3.1], [3.2]). For discovery-breadth endpoints, **family-first diversity-aware acquisition** fills a gap unaddressed by standard methods.

**Critical methodological notes.** Multi-fidelity transfer learning in molecular discovery achieves up to 8× improvement on sparse tasks but only when low-fidelity sources are informative and the transfer representation is well-designed; standard transfer approaches fail with naive readout functions ([5.1], [5.2]). Similarly, Sabanza-Gil et al. find that MFBO helps only when auxiliary sources are both informative (high R²) and cheap (low ρ); when these conditions are absent, MFBO provides little benefit over single-fidelity BO ([4.1]). This aligns with the project's finding that borrowing is not universally beneficial and reinforces the need for explicit source-quality assessment.

## E. Synthesis

### E1. Strongest Defensible Positive Central Claim

*Under strict confirmatory controls, within-campaign neighboring-condition edges with small condition gaps, high source credibility, and exact provenance exclusions can improve data-poor target-region prediction in experimental materials science—but this benefit is local (not global), directional, endpoint-specific, and must be embedded in an abstaining framework that reports null and harmful edges as part of the scientific map rather than suppressing them.* The KIT conductivity case (15% RMSE reduction at n=30, passing all controls) provides the strongest single piece of evidence, consistent with multi-fidelity transfer literature showing that informative, cheap auxiliaries help while unreliable ones hurt ([3.1], [4.1], [5.1]). The Borg→BIRDSHOT failure (R²=−3.006) provides equally strong evidence for the negative side, consistent with benchmark findings that even strong in-distribution laws fail OOD when the chemistry-property relationship changes fundamentally ([1.1], [1.3], [11.2]).

### E2. Largest Unresolved Confound

**Leakage through implicit representation sharing.** Even with exact identity exclusion, if source and target share a representation space constructed with knowledge of both datasets, the representation itself may encode target-distributional information that inflates apparent transfer utility. The project correctly requires source cross-fitting, but the larger question of whether the feature engineering implicitly conditions on target-reachable chemistry has not been subjected to a fully independent representation audit. This is the single confound most likely to diminish effect sizes upon strict replication.

### E3. Minimum Additional Evidence for a High-Impact Methods Paper

Three elements are needed: **1** One prospective outcome-unseen replication of the neighboring-condition edge on a genuinely independent campaign (Experiment 1 above). **2** A formal abstention/borrowing-gate evaluation across at least 15 edges demonstrating reduced harmful-transfer rate versus always-borrow (Experiment 3 above). **3** One prospective multi-neighbor portfolio demonstration recovering external hits with preregistered endpoints (Experiment 4 above). Without 1, the single confirmed edge remains anecdotal. Without 2, the abstention thesis lacks operational validation. Without 3, the portfolio claim remains retrospective. The compensation-law analysis and boundary-programme nulls are already valuable as negative results and need no additional experiments—they strengthen the paper if framed as limits of naive aggregation rather than failures of the overall thesis.

### E5. Revised Paper Narrative

**Title direction:** "When Neighbors Help: A Directed, Abstaining Knowledge-Borrowing Map for Data-Poor Materials Decisions"

**Revised narrative.** The paper should open not with the failure of universal laws (which is well-established) but with the **positive observation** that experimental materials science generates vast quantities of near-neighbor data that are routinely discarded because there is no safe, disciplined protocol for reusing them. The KIT case concretely demonstrates that a within-campaign neighbor 10°C away provides a 15% RMSE reduction and 37% label savings—a finding consistent with multi-fidelity transfer literature showing that sufficiently informative, low-cost auxiliaries reliably help ([4.1], [5.1]). The Borg→BIRDSHOT failure then demonstrates the danger: a seemingly strong law (R²=0.79 within domain) becomes catastrophic when transported (R²=−3.006). This contrast motivates the central methodological contribution: an operational framework that formalizes who can lend knowledge to whom, for which endpoint, in which target region, and when the system must refuse.

The narrative should then structure the results around the endpoint taxonomy (prediction, screening, acquisition, region discovery), showing that the same edge can succeed on one endpoint and fail on another—a finding unique to this work. The compensation-law analysis and boundary-programme nulls should be positioned as evidence *for* the abstention thesis rather than as failures: they demonstrate that the map correctly identifies edges where borrowing should not proceed, consistent with the well-documented prevalence of negative transfer ([2.1], [2.2]) and the artifact susceptibility of pooled regularities ([16.1], [16.2]). The portfolio demonstration on the Caltech target should be acknowledged as retrospective but presented as hypothesis-generating for the complementary-rankings mechanism.

The paper should conclude with the borrowing map itself as the primary intellectual contribution—a directed, weighted, abstaining graph over experimental resources—and with preregistered next experiments (this report's Deliverable C) as the prospective validation plan. This framing is ambitious because it claims a new scientific instrument (the map) rather than merely a specific transfer result, while remaining rigorous because it reports failures and abstentions as scientific output.

**What to avoid.** The narrative should not claim that neighboring-domain transfer is universally beneficial (it is not), that the portfolio result is confirmed (it is retrospective), or that the data infrastructure alone constitutes a sufficient contribution (118 resources and 96K measurements are impressive but not publishable without the scientific question they serve). The strongest version of the paper is one that makes the KIT-type local transfer the empirical anchor, the abstention framework the methodological anchor, and the directed map the conceptual anchor—then uses the nulls and failures as evidence that the map works because it correctly identifies where borrowing should not proceed.
