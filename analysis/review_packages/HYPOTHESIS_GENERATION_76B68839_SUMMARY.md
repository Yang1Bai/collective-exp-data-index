## Abstract

This research programme establishes a formal framework for "selective knowledge-borrowing" within the domains of experimental chemistry and materials science, addressing the persistent challenge of negative transfer in machine learning. Unlike generic transfer-learning models that assume global domain alignment, this approach introduces a directed, abstaining map of chemical and material spaces designed to identify specific "harmful edges" where information transfer must be refused to prevent performance degradation. Key findings highlight the efficacy of a Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) system integrated with physical consistency audits. Most notably, the programme identifies Physics-Based Zero-Shot Oracle Gates (PZOG) as a promising solution to the "Target Gradient Paradox," ensuring borrowed knowledge adheres to first-principles laws—such as ligand field theory and the Sabatier principle—even in data-sparse target regions. Additional innovations include "softmax-free" routing to enable total principled abstention and the use of shuffling-sensitivity as a diagnostic heuristic for determining appropriate representational depth. The framework successfully bridges the gap between statistical correlation and mechanistic grounding, treating negative transfer as a physical signal that defines the boundaries of chemical theories.

The recommended path forward prioritizes the implementation of the Directed Abstention Map (Idea 1) integrated with symbolic physical oracle mechanisms. Immediate next steps involve the operationalization of a transition-metal-inclusive data pipeline and a retrospective benchmarking phase utilizing high-throughput experimentation (HTE) datasets for palladium-catalyzed couplings. This will be followed by a preregistered prospective validation phase targeting complex materials discovery, specifically investigating negative thermal expansion in the BaIrO3 iridate family and coordination-driven spin states in Fe-porphyrinic frameworks. Success will be measured through quantifiable metrics, primarily the Sample-Savings Ratio (SSR) and 1% Enrichment Factors, to definitively validate the programme’s capacity for principled, out-of-distribution transfer.
