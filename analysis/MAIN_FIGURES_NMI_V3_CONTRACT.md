# NMI-v3 contracts for main quantitative figures

## Figure 2: failure establishes the transfer boundary

**Core conclusion.** A relation can be genuine inside one experimental
programme yet fail across provenance, and generic donor-feature injection does
not repair a recipient's far-OOD region.

**Archetype.** Asymmetric quantitative grid.

**Panel map.**

- **a, hero evidence:** a strong alloy relation, its independent recipient
  refit, and the failed unchanged coefficient transport.
- **b, portfolio evidence:** relative far-OOD RMSE effects for all 40 real
  donor-recipient edges, with the declared donor column visually fixed.
- **c, decision audit:** the five conjunctive gate criteria for the eight
  declared edges and the resulting 0/40 complete-pass verdict.

**Evidence hierarchy.** Failed coefficient transport first; portfolio-scale
failure second; gate-level diagnosis third.

**Reviewer risk.** A positive relative error change must not be confused with
useful absolute prediction. The figure must retain the negative augmented
OOD-R2 requirement in the legend and the complete conjunction in panel c.

## Figure 3: a qualified relation can support numerical OOD prediction

**Core conclusion.** Borrowing becomes useful when the transferred object is an
explicit, state-compatible relation that survives matched falsifiers, including
an external recipient containing a salt absent from the source.

**Archetype.** Asymmetric mixed-modality figure with one quantitative hero.

**Panel map.**

- **a, system definition:** 22 source salts, a component-order-invariant
  relation and an external unseen-salt recipient.
- **b, hero evidence:** row-level external prediction for all 1,827 measurements
  with raw R2, log-scale R2 and Spearman correlation stated separately.
- **c, falsification:** formulation-grouped bootstrap effects against the state,
  chemistry-permuted and salt-removal controls.
- **d, controlled selectivity:** four complete catalyst-system perturbations
  routed to predict-and-rank, ranking only or rejection.

**Evidence hierarchy.** External unseen-salt prediction first; matched
falsifiers second; controlled heterogeneous outcomes third.

**Reviewer risk.** The catalyst composition relation was selected after target
inspection and must remain labelled post-primary. The external benchmark is
retrospective. Raw-scale R2=0.629 must not be replaced by log-scale R2=0.732.

## Figure 4: useful ordering is not equivalent to calibrated prediction

**Core conclusion.** A neighbouring-programme score can recover OOD candidate
order from five recipient measurements, but endpoint and programme boundaries
route the same family of knowledge to screening or abstention.

**Archetype.** Asymmetric quantitative grid.

**Panel map.**

- **a, system definition:** three independently trained source programmes,
  equal programme weight, five anchors and separate prediction/screening routes.
- **b, hero evidence:** the programme score versus 13 recipient-only models and
  a non-deployable per-draw oracle.
- **c, sparse-data robustness:** candidate-order correlation at three, five and
  ten measured recipient formulations.
- **d, boundary test:** the accepted primary recipient and frozen second
  recipient shown on one effect-size axis.

**Evidence hierarchy.** Five-anchor benchmark first; anchor-budget robustness
second; frozen programme boundary third.

**Reviewer risk.** The score is not an absolute conductivity prediction. The
frozen second-recipient rejection must be as visible as the primary success,
and the source programme must not be described as universally transferable.

## Shared export and style contract

- Python/Matplotlib only, 183 mm double-column width, maximum height below 170
  mm.
- Arial, 5-7 pt body text, 8 pt bold lowercase panel labels.
- Editable PDF/SVG, 300 dpi PNG and 600 dpi LZW-compressed TIFF.
- Navy/blue: measured recipient evidence; teal: accepted borrowed signal;
  orange: ranking-only or held-out candidates; coral: harm or rejection; grey:
  recipient baselines and construction lines.
- One dominant panel per figure, direct labels where stable, no redundant
  legends, and exact source-data tables for every quantitative annotation.
