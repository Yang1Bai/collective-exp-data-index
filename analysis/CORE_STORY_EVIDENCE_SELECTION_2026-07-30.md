# Core-story evidence selection

## One-sentence argument

When recipient experiments are too sparse to support reliable out-of-distribution
(OOD) decisions, neighbouring experimental programmes can supply useful missing
knowledge, but only when the donor--recipient relation, experimental state,
transferred object, leakage boundary, and decision endpoint are matched and the
edge is allowed to abstain.

## Main-text evidence

| Narrative job | Evidence retained in the main text | Why it is indispensable |
|---|---|---|
| Show why the problem is non-trivial | No generic donor-feature edge passed the complete OOD-repair gate among 40 declared edges across eight targets | Rules out the easy explanation that adding any physically adjacent prediction helps |
| Show controlled predictive transfer | One 462-catalyst oxygen-evolution donor transferred to four complete 126-catalyst derivative systems; with five recipient labels, RMSE fell by 16.3% and 26.1% in two systems, one system was ranking-only, and one was harmful | Provides a controlled perturbation series containing positive, endpoint-specific, and rejected edges under the same assay |
| Show cross-database absolute prediction | A permutation-invariant relation trained on 10,407 electrolyte measurements from 22 salts predicted an external unseen-salt programme with raw \(R^2=0.629\), \(\rho=0.871\), and 28.64% lower log-RMSE than a state-only model | Demonstrates that a scientifically matched relation can cross database and component identity |
| Show cross-database OOD screening | With five recipient formulations, a three-programme source score ranked unseen formulations at \(\rho=0.910\), versus 0.537 for the strongest of 13 recipient-only configurations; \(\Delta\rho=0.374\), 95% interval 0.213--0.562 | Strongest evidence that borrowed experimental knowledge rescues a data-poor recipient decision |
| Establish the boundary | The same frozen ordinal route lost to the three-anchor recipient model in a second electrolyte programme | Prevents the positive result from becoming a universal-transfer claim and makes abstention part of the method |

## Supplementary-only evidence

The following analyses remain scientifically useful but no longer carry the
main narrative:

- within-programme neighbouring-temperature conductivity transfer;
- post-outcome cross-article response-relation transfer;
- state-aware alloy strength transfer and its provenance-specificity controls;
- fixed screening and sequential acquisition in the solid-electrolyte target;
- static ranking experiments in the Caltech electrolyte target;
- outcome-unseen reverse-transport and second-family catalysis nulls;
- detailed thermoelectric and adsorption compensation-law audits;
- complete resource-by-resource cohort accounting and every failed donor edge.

These results should be cited from the main text only when they define a gate,
support a robustness statement, or explain why an edge was rejected. They
should not be presented as additional showcases.

## Claim boundary

The paper demonstrates retrospective OOD prediction and candidate-ranking
improvements in selected experimental programmes. It does not demonstrate a
universal transfer rule, prospective laboratory discovery acceleration, or a
new physical law.
