# Main-figure QA record

## Claim and archetype

**Core conclusion.** Aggregated regularities and source-domain calibrations do
not automatically transport, whereas a leakage-safe adjacent-condition prior
can cross every originally specified point-rule gate within a controlled campaign; the benefit decays
with physical distance and is destroyed by feature scrambling. A paper-
disjoint CALiSol test fails to reproduce the improvement or distance ordering.

The figure is a four-panel quantitative evidence chain: external transport
falsification, local-neighbor effects plus a cross-article boundary,
absolute sample-efficiency improvement, and a pooled-law condition/artifact diagnostic. All
panels, previews, and exports are generated by the Python/matplotlib backend in
`make_main_knowledge_map_figure.py`.

## Statistical legend record

### Panel a: direct strength-law transport

- `n`: 495 paired Borg records from 208 canonical compositions and 171 paired
  BIRDSHOT records from 151 canonical compositions.
- Center/model: ordinary least-squares line in log10 UTS--yield-strength space.
- Uncertainty/test: 5,000 composition-cluster bootstrap replicates; the panel
  reports in-domain and unchanged external R2 values rather than a pointwise
  confidence band.
- Split/leakage: zero exact canonical-composition overlap between datasets.

### Panel b: adjacent-temperature borrowing, controls, and transport boundary

- Independent unit: 108 complete liquid-electrolyte formulations after
  aggregating 5,035 runs from 504 experiment IDs by the formulation-temperature
  median.
- Split: five balanced outer folds grouped by exact formulation; every
  formulation is tested once per repeat. Source models exclude the outer-test
  formulations, and source predictions for target-training formulations are
  fivefold cross-fitted.
- Target budget/model: 30 -30 degC formulation labels per fold; primary target
  and source learners are 300-tree random forests with minimum leaf size 2.
- Metric/baseline: relative reduction in held-out log10-conductivity RMSE versus
  an otherwise identical composition-only target model. Positive values favor
  borrowing.
- Intervals: 5,000-replicate hierarchical bootstrap over target-sampling
  repeats and held-out formulations within outer folds.
- Primary test: 999 one-sided fixed-subset source-feature mappings; p=0.001.
- Robustness: all five outer-fold mean effects are positive; random forest,
  ExtraTrees, and degree-2 Ridge sensitivities are all positive.
- Controls: source temperatures 0, 30, and 60 degC were frozen as increasing-
  distance controls; a permuted -20 degC source-label model is the placebo.
  Effects decrease strictly with target-source temperature distance, and the
  shuffled feature is harmful.
- Independent boundary: CALiSol contributes 891 eligible target formulations
  from 15 articles. Five outer folds hold out complete articles; source models
  exclude every test article and every exact test chemistry identity, and
  development priors are leave-one-article-out.
- CALiSol interval: the -30 to -40 degC primary changes RMSE by 1.61% with an
  article-hierarchical 95% interval of -2.14% to +4.21%. Pooled R2 remains
  negative (-0.049 to -0.014), two folds are harmful, and the 0 degC control is
  numerically stronger. The figure therefore labels practical borrowing as failed rather
  than promoting the fixed-subset mapping p=0.004.

### Panel c: target-label equivalence

- Baseline curve: target-only random forest at 8, 15, 30, 45, 60, and 75
  -30 degC labels. Non-primary budgets use 60 target-sampling repeats; n=30 is
  anchored to the exact 100 primary baseline predictions.
- Center/spread: mean held-out RMSE and one standard deviation over sampling
  repeats. The plotted augmented point uses the same 100 primary repeats.
- Equivalence: monotone interpolation maps the augmented n=30 RMSE to a point
  estimate of 47.884 baseline labels, corresponding to 37.35% of equivalent
  target labels saved. A post-outcome 5,000-replicate formulation/subset
  bootstrap gives n=38.38--59.89 and 21.84--49.91% saved; 80.52% of replicates
  meet the frozen 30% point threshold.
- Scope: simulated label poverty within one experimental campaign; the
  diagnostic is conditional on this campaign and does not establish an
  uncertainty-qualified 30% lower bound or independent-dataset rescue. The
  target-only n=30 baseline R² is already 0.739, so the figure supports material
  improvement rather than conversion of an unusable task into a usable one.

### Panel d: ISODB isosteric compensation

- `n`: 1,103 one-fit-per-system estimates from 512 DOI clusters.
- Center/model: pooled ordinary least-squares line; slope inference uses HC3
  standard errors. The pooled R2 is 0.637.
- Uncertainty/artifact tests: 2,000 DOI-cluster bootstrap replicates and 1,000
  independent-parameter Krug-null simulations.
- Condition test: adsorbate-family intercept comparison uses a 4,999-replicate
  DOI wild-cluster test (p=0.0002). Family-specific slope heterogeneity is not
  established by the corresponding clustered test (p=0.625).

## OOD decision figure QA

### Claim and archetype

**Core conclusion.** A directional fixed-ranking OOD signal does not imply
sequential discovery acceleration. Under the frozen OBELiX campaign, the
thermoelectric prior does not improve the tested UCB policy, while the
prespecified uniform-random reference is substantially faster.

The figure is an asymmetric quantitative grid. Panels a and b separate fixed
screening from sequential effect estimates; panel c is the hero cumulative-
discovery trajectory. All rendering and exports are generated with the Python/
matplotlib backend in `make_ood_decision_figure.py`.

### Statistical legend record

#### Panel a: fixed OOD screening

- Official pool: 110 canonical, DOI/composition-disjoint OBELiX test
  compositions; 60 repeated n=30 target subsets.
- Endpoint: one-indexed rank of the first observed top-5% candidate divided by
  pool size. Lower is better.
- Official result: 12.08% target-only versus 9.98% with the thermoelectric
  prior; 2.09 percentage points saved [0.94,3.45], Holm p=0.0003.
- Hard OOD: fixed farthest 40% by outcome-free elemental-fraction L1 distance;
  21.59% versus 18.11%, 3.48 percentage points saved [1.74,5.42]. It is
  exploratory because its design followed the whole-pool direction.

#### Panel b: sequential effects

- Primary policy: 80-tree ExtraTrees, ensemble mean plus one ensemble standard
  deviation, refitted after every acquisition; 100 paired seeds and a
  40-acquisition budget.
- Endpoint: target-only minus comparator acquisitions to the first true top-5%
  hit; no-hit campaigns are censored at 41. Positive values favor the
  comparator.
- Intervals: 5,000-replicate paired percentile bootstrap. Tests use 9,999
  paired sign flips in the prespecified beneficial direction.
- Official thermoelectric result: +0.25 [−1.30,1.82], p=0.3889. Hard-OOD
  thermoelectric result: +2.21 [0.97,3.49], p=0.0004, but the five-experiment,
  25%, 60%-consistency, and Random-Forest-sensitivity gates fail.
- Random acquisition: +8.84 [4.57,12.90] official and +6.12 [3.41,8.87]
  hard-OOD experiments relative to target-only UCB.

#### Panel c: cumulative discovery trajectory

- Curves show the empirical fraction of 100 official-test campaigns that have
  reached a true top-5% candidate by each acquisition.
- Median acquisitions are 28 target-only, 26 thermoelectric-prior, and 13
  random. Censoring at acquisition 40 is 40%, 41%, and 8%, respectively.
- Scope: retrospective held-out-pool simulation. Random superiority establishes
  failure of the tested UCB policies here, not the cause of failure, general
  optimality of random search, or prospective laboratory acceleration.

## Caltech external-policy decomposition QA record

### Claim and archetype

**Core conclusion.** Prespecified real-neighbor rankings retain external OOD
signal after exact formula and DOI exclusions, and the admission rule suppresses
wrong-domain weight. None of the target-refitted adaptive source increments
improves acquisition, so source signal and policy conversion must be treated as
separate design problems. Weak source and target backbones prevent clean
attribution of the adaptive failure.

The figure is a three-panel quantitative validation grid. Panel c shows
prespecified retrospective external ranking evidence, panel a shows selective
gate behavior and the source-skill caveat, and panel b carries the adaptive
policy boundary. All rendering and exports are
generated with the Python/matplotlib backend in
`make_caltech_external_policy_figure.py`.

### Statistical legend record

#### Panel a: admission versus source skill

- Means combine the complete 144-candidate external scope and frozen
  58-candidate hard-OOD scope.
- Each single-source policy contributes 4,000 evaluated acquisition steps per
  scope. Circles show admission rate; open squares show mean source weight.
- Real-neighbor mean admission/weight are 0.355/0.168, compared with
  0.168/0.063 for wrong controls. All wrong-source harm guards pass; the Borg
  hard-OOD admission rate of 0.1965 lies narrowly below the frozen 0.20 ceiling.
- Source OOF R² values are 0.065 for OBELiX, 0.257 for ESTM, 0.164 for Borg,

#### Panel b: adaptive source increments

  policy over 100 paired campaigns in each scope.
- Points are mean AUC20 gains and horizontal lines are paired-bootstrap 95%
  intervals. Tests use paired sign flips with Holm correction over eight
  contrasts per scope.
- External/hard-OOD gains are −0.06 [−0.30,0.12]/+1.10 [−0.38,2.59] for
  OBELiX, −0.06 [−0.23,0.07]/−0.07 [−1.11,0.91] for ESTM, and −0.09
  [−0.34,0.09]/+1.27 [−0.75,3.22] for multisource residuals. None passes all
  frozen gates.

#### Panel c: static source rankings

- Bars show AUC20 for deterministic static rankings in the complete external
  and hard-OOD scopes. Candidate-seed repetition is not interpreted as
  independent-dataset uncertainty.
- OBELiX/ESTM AUC20 values are 33/45 externally and 38/51 in hard OOD, compared
  with random values 11.25/9.87. Their recall20 counts are 2/8 and 3/8
  externally and 3/3 for both in the n-limited hard-OOD scope. Both real neighbors exceed shuffled,
  mechanical, and catalysis controls in both scopes.
- Static policies were prespecified, but source-attribution comparisons and
  decision gates were not in the frozen primary family. The panel is
  descriptive and excludes the outcome-selected neighbor portfolio.

## CCA family-first exploration QA record

### Claim and archetype

**Core conclusion.** Preserving qualified neighbor ranks and diversifying the
first acquisition pass across outcome-free identity/provenance components
recovers broader high-value target regions than entity consensus. This breadth
gain trades away repeated within-component entity hits and is outcome-informed
method development, not prospective discovery.

The figure is a Python/matplotlib schematic-led composite: panel a defines the
CCA policy, panels b and c show complete-pool and hard-OOD distinct-component
AUC20, and panel d exposes the breadth-versus-repeat trade-off.

### Export and visual QA

- Export bundle: editable SVG and PDF, 300 dpi PNG preview, and a 600 dpi TIFF.
- Final width: 7.2 inches (182.9 mm), within the 183 mm double-column contract.
- One restrained palette distinguishes target-only baselines, wrong sources,
  individual neighbors, entity consensus, and family-first consensus; labels
  and ordering preserve meaning without color.
- Original-resolution PNG inspection found no clipped panel labels, axes,
  method names, values, or footnotes after horizontal-bar and direct-label
  revisions.
- Source data are `family_first_neighbor_portfolio_metrics.csv` and
  `family_first_neighbor_portfolio_summary.json`; all marks are regenerated by
  `make_family_first_neighbor_portfolio_figure.py`.

### Statistical legend record

- External pool: 144 entities in 63 connected formula/DOI/ICSD components; top
  ceil(5%) gives four components. Family-first consensus AUC20=60 and recall
  4/4; entity consensus AUC20=47 and recall 3/4; wrong-pair AUC20=6.
- Hard-OOD pool: 58 entities in 36 components; top ceil(5%) gives two
  components. Family-first AUC20=39 with both components at acquisitions 1 and
  2; wrong-pair AUC20=18.
- Grey bands and dashed lines show the 2.5--97.5% range and mean from 5,000
  independent within-pool permutations of both real-neighbor ranks. Conditional
  p-values are 0.0020 externally and 0.0030 in hard OOD. These are fixed-pool
  randomization results, not dataset-level intervals.
- Candidate-outcome permutation leaves every policy order unchanged.
- Family-first entity recall20 is 2/8 externally and 1/3 in hard OOD, versus
  5/8 and 3/3 for entity consensus; this reduction is displayed rather than
  hidden.
