# Next decisive experiment: preregistered external relation-transfer challenge

## Update after the physics-aware electrolyte benchmark

The newly implemented mixture relation should now be frozen as a second
confirmatory option. In the retrospective external LiAsF6 benchmark it achieved
zero-shot raw \(R^2=0.629\) and Spearman \(\rho=0.871\), reduced log-RMSE by
28.64% [24.03%,33.52%] versus a state-only model, and beat state-only error and
ranking in 7 of 9 leave-one-salt-out targets. Five target anchors did not
create this signal: the frozen source relation already strongly outperformed a
same-budget target-only model, while a shrinkage adapter mainly corrected
absolute scale.

Accordingly, a newly measured or outcome-sealed electrolyte programme is now
the most direct computational confirmation route. Freeze the exact
permutation-invariant representation, source salts, Random Forest
hyperparameters, formulation-level split, shrinkage adapter and three
falsifiers before outcome access. The primary comparison is source relation
versus same-label-budget target-only prediction; state-only,
chemistry-permuted and closest-neighbour-excluded sources remain mandatory.
The OER experiment below remains the preferred prospective laboratory route.

## Why this is the only high-leverage next experiment

The retrospective evidence now answers the method-development question:
generic feature injection fails, a composition–OER relation rescues two of four
controlled derivative systems, one cross-database ordinal edge is strong, and
both mechanisms have explicit null or harmful neighbors. Searching additional
deposited databases would mainly add post-selection risk. The unresolved claim
is whether a qualified relation can improve a real, outcome-unseen decision in
an experimental programme independent of the donor study.

## Claim to test

A relation frozen from a physically adjacent experimental system improves
prediction or enriches a recipient's high-value OOD shortlist relative to a
recipient-only policy, without using recipient candidate outcomes for donor
selection or method choice.

## Minimal design

1. Select one recipient programme with 24–40 feasible, unmeasured candidates,
   one fixed measurement protocol, and a decision-relevant endpoint.
2. Require one donor from a different but mechanistically adjacent property,
   with positive grouped source out-of-fold skill and at least 80% candidate
   support. Freeze its model and candidate scores.
3. Measure four outcome-blind, space-filling recipient anchors. Use them only
   to test score orientation and gross programme incompatibility; do not refit
   the donor.
4. If anchor pairwise concordance is below 0.5, abstain before candidate
   selection. Otherwise allocate candidates equally to:
   - frozen donor ranking;
   - recipient-only model fitted to the four anchors;
   - composition novelty;
   - uniform random reference.
5. Measure 6–8 candidates per arm under the same protocol. Deduplicate any
   candidate selected by more than one arm before outcome access and preserve
   arm attribution.

## Frozen primary endpoint

Top-quartile enrichment among measured candidates, with normalized regret as a
co-primary practical metric. Absolute prediction error is secondary because
the transferred object is an ordinal score.

## Success gate

The donor arm must:

- exceed the recipient-only arm by at least 0.20 in top-quartile precision;
- reduce normalized regret by at least 25%;
- beat both composition novelty and the prespecified shuffled-donor null;
- retain the same direction under leave-one-candidate-out sensitivity;
- pass identity, article, and measurement-protocol leakage audits.

Failure or anchor-stage abstention remains a valid result and cannot trigger a
replacement donor.

## Preferred scientific instantiation

The lowest-risk confirmatory design is a new high-entropy OER derivative
programme whose six composition slots and electrochemical endpoint are fixed
before measurement. Freeze the present donor relation, reveal five
space-filling recipient anchors, and compare anchored donor correction with a
target-only model on 24–40 remaining candidates. The scientifically broader,
higher-risk alternative is a transport-adjacent donor used to rank a sealed
ionic-conductivity candidate pool. The OER study tests reproducibility of the
new method; the cross-property study tests how far the neighborhood can extend.

## What this would change

A pass would convert the paper from a retrospective routing framework into
prospective evidence that neighboring-property knowledge improves OOD
experimental selection. A null would retain the present paper's selective-map
claim. An abstention would validate the safety logic but not transfer utility.
